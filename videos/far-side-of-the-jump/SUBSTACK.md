**Title:** Eyes on the Far Side
**Subtitle:** A riding lesson for the age of AI doom trackers

---

My first riding teacher had silver-white hair at thirty and one instruction she repeated until it stuck: look at the far side of the jump. Stare at the pole and the pony stares at it too. Lift your chin to something past the fence and you both sail over.

I keep hearing her in this season of AI doom trackers. The best of them are rigorous. [Failsafe](https://failsafe.developai.co.za/) and the [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/) log failures with their sources and the people who caught them, and that discipline deserves copying. It rarely gets pointed at the far side of the fence.

[UtopAI](https://www.khayali.xyz/utopai) points it there. It keeps a small, sourced record of what people already make possible with AI, each entry with its evidence and its limits: flood forecasts reaching rivers that have no gauges, photos described aloud for people who can’t see them, a grandmother’s stories kept as searchable text, African-language research led by its speakers. Alongside the record sits a studio that turns “what would you love to make possible?” into one small experiment you can try this week.

And now there’s a song.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-pmjEYgoEo

## How it was made

Suno made the music from a prompt and lyrics I wrote with Claude. Codex painted the girl, her spotted mare, the purple teacher and the glass garden, then repainted each scene as separate transparent layers. Claude, working in Claude Code with HyperFrames, turned those layers into a video by writing HTML and code: parallax camera moves, a four-frame gallop cycle, lyrics timed to a transcript of the vocals, and a glass tree that grows and blooms on the actual beats of the track. No video model touched it. My laptop rendered all 8,267 frames.

Who did what: I brought the idea and the quality checks. Claude did most of the directing, from the storyboard to the hungry jump-pole gag. I caught two things a rider would notice. The girl gripped her reins like bicycle handlebars, and the small text flew past too fast to read. Both got fixed.

What would you love to make possible? Say it out loud. Then try something small.
